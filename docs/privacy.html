Nexus privacy policy

Last updated 12 November 2026.

Nexus is a personal app used by its developer on their own devices. It is not offered to anyone else.

What Nexus accesses

When you connect a Google account, Nexus asks for permission to:

  • Google Calendar: read your calendar list, and read, create, change and delete events, so your calendar shows in Nexus and edits made in Nexus reach Google Calendar.
  • Google Docs: read and edit the Google Docs you link to Nexus, so your notes and those Docs stay the same, comments and suggestions included.
  • Google Drive (only files Nexus made or you opened with it): list the earlier versions of those Docs, so Nexus can show their history.

Where your data goes

Everything Nexus reads from Google is stored only on your own computers and devices, in your own data folder and on your own home server that keeps them in sync. Your Google sign-in is kept encrypted on your computer, and a copy is kept on your own home server so it can sync Docs while your laptop is off.

Nexus has no servers of its own. It does not send your Google data to the developer or anyone else, does not sell it, does not use it for advertising, and does not use it to train AI models. There are no analytics or tracking.

Google API Services User Data Policy

Nexus's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Removing access

Disconnect the account in Nexus (Settings › Calendars or Settings › Sync), or remove Nexus at myaccount.google.com/permissions. Deleting your Nexus data folder deletes everything Nexus kept.

Contact

Use the support email shown on Google's sign-in screen for this app.
