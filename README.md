# Nexus mobile

The iPad and iPhone side of [Nexus](https://github.com/DINOcoder2910), a personal, file-based workspace. Only the app's code lives here; notes never do.

## What it does now

One app for the iPad and the iPhone. On the iPhone the sidebar is the first screen of a stack (it opens on Today, with the sidebar one step back), the Planner's days are stacked instead of side by side, and a board draws with the finger (there is no Pencil). Every feature the iPad has is on the iPhone too, laid out for it: a board's **Split screen** stacks the second pane (Today, the Calendar, another board or a PDF) under the board, with a divider to drag up and down.

- **The planner side of Nexus**, on the same data folder as the laptop: a sidebar with Today, Planner, Calendar, Tasks, the workstreams by group, Inbox and Someday.
- **The new look** (Nov 20 2026, the laptop's too): tones instead of lines, Gabarito titles and Onest text, one spring for every motion. In the sidebar each subject opens as a folder with its arrow (Tasks, its notebooks, Study, Files) or, by its name, steps inside it: only that subject, the places as small icons on top, Back to every subject; on the iPad Claude, Ideas, Time, Files, Settings and the guide are icons at its foot. A subject has its own page (next up, Study, notebooks, files). On the iPhone the Notebooks tab is now **Subjects**.
- **The board's bar**: the board's name, a Draw | Insert | View switch, then a row with that choice (Draw: the tools, every colour and size in view); ︿ folds it into one bar (remembered). The **quick ring** floats at the edge: tap for a fan of tools, colours and sizes or hold and slide onto one, double tap for the last tool, drag it to move it or push it into the edge to tuck it away; it fades while you write (View › Quick ring turns it off). **Your pens are the laptop's**: each ink's colour and size, the colour rows, the sizes set on the laptop (right-click a size there) and the eraser come from the data folder's `nexus-pens.json` and a change here goes back into it a moment later (NexusCore `Pens.swift`, `Sources/Boards/BoardTools.swift`; the laptop's sizes are drawn in proportion to the iPad's own, since PencilKit's inks are not the laptop's). The tool in hand, the bar, the ring and the shapes' colour stay each device's own.
  - **Home**: the laptop's first screen: the greeting and where the day stands, the key dates, the Inbox while it has something (tick, add, open it), and Where things stand (slipped, waiting on, workstreams; tap one to go there). The iPad lays it out as the laptop does; the iPhone puts the three figures first as one row.
  - **Today**: the plan (times first), repeats that fall today, deadlines that need you, what slipped (with Today and Move all to today), tomorrow, and the key dates ahead.
  - **Planner**: 3 days or a week, slipped tasks on top, the task bank below; drag a task onto a day.
  - **Calendar**: the device's own calendars through EventKit (whatever accounts are added in iOS Settings: Google, the school's Microsoft 365, iCloud, subscribed ICS links) with the tasks planned at a time and the deadlines. Day, 3 days, Week (on the iPhone drawn narrow: the weekday over the date, smaller blocks), Month and List; tap an event for the system's event sheet (edit, delete), double-tap an empty time for a new event, drag a task's block to plan it at another time. **Place tasks** lists the unplanned tasks by when they are due (a drawer to drag from on the iPad, a sheet on the iPhone; tap one to pick a day and a time). Today and the Planner show the day's events too. Nothing of it goes into the data folder (the laptop keeps its calendar sync in its own profile too, `docs/areas.md` "Calendar").
  - **Tasks**: the laptop's lenses (All, Deadlines, Waiting on, No date, Slipped, Done, Someday) and search.
  - **A workstream's page**: its task tree, its study decks, Someday and Done, its notes.md (add or edit it) and its files.
  - **Study**: the memorize decks of every workstream with a `study/decks` folder (made on the laptop, here through sync) and what is due. A review is flip and grade, type the answer (SO4 2-, SO4^2- and SO₄²⁻ all count) or multiple choice, reversible decks either way, Spanish (flip) decks with the side shown first, a tag and new cards a day. Scheduling is the laptop's FSRS (NexusCore `StudyFSRS`, checked against the laptop's own code), and each grade goes into the workstream's `study/review.yaml` at once, merged card by card with what is on disk, so reviews on the laptop, iPad and iPhone all count. A conflict copy the hub makes of that file is folded in and removed. Practice classifying, units and sheets stay on the laptop.
  - **The task panel**: everything is editable: title, ★, deadline, planned days and times, estimate, subtasks, waiting on, repeats, tags, the note, Someday.
  - **Add a task** anywhere with +, into a workstream or the Inbox; a typed repeat, waiting on and #tags are written too.
- **Files**: the data folder as it is on disk (sidebar, or More on the iPhone). PDFs, pictures, Word, Excel, PowerPoint, text and YAML open in the system's viewer, a folder in place, a board as a board; hold a file to share, rename or delete it (to `.trash`, which never syncs); + makes a folder or brings files in from the Files app. The files Nexus is built on and boards show a lock: their own screens rename and delete them.
- **How Nexus works**: the laptop's guide in each device's words (sidebar, or More on the iPhone).
- **Claude**, the laptop's Claude panel (Settings › Claude: your plan through the hub, or your API key; the Plan / API chip beside each Claude feature flips it). Claude reads the data folder and proposes each change as a card with its changed lines, to **Accept** or **Reject** (Undo after); nothing is written without your Accept. It can't run tools on the device, so it asks with small tags the app answers (`<read>`, `<list>`, `<search>`, `<edit>`, `<write>`; NexusCore `ClaudeChat`), inside the folder only, never `.git/` or `.nexus/`. The model menu (Sonnet, Haiku, Opus), New conversation and Earlier conversations (the last 15, kept on the device), and the commands (Plan my days, File my Inbox, Weekly review, New meeting note, Tidy loose ends).
  - **iPad**: the ✦ button in any screen's toolbar opens the conversation as a column beside it (told which screen you are on); the sidebar's **Claude** row shows it full width. The model menu, the chip and the commands are in view.
  - **iPhone**: the **Claude** row in More, and a sheet from Today's ✦ menu, a task's **Ask Claude**, or a workstream's ⋯ menu. Only the chip is in view: the model, New conversation and Earlier conversations are behind ⋯, the commands behind the / button beside the box.
  - **Plan with Claude** (Home, both devices; Today: in view on the iPad, in the ✦ menu on the iPhone): Claude places what slipped into today's free time (around your calendar and the tasks planned at a time). Each placement on today is saved at once, with one Undo for the run; any other change waits in the conversation. **File my Inbox** (the Inbox's ⋯ menu, and Home's Inbox card) proposes where each Inbox item goes.
  - **The day brief**: the laptop's Home brief (what is due, what slipped, what has gone quiet), written by Claude once a day with a cheap model from the plans alone (no files read), kept on the device for the day, with Refresh; the plain summary when it is off (Settings › Claude) or Claude can't be reached. It shows on an empty conversation and on Home.
- **From outside the app**: **Add to Nexus** for Siri ("Add to Nexus"), the Shortcuts app and the Action button (the line is read as the add box reads it and synced at once), and **New Nexus task** to open the add sheet. Open tasks, workstreams and boards are in **Spotlight**.
- **Ideas** (the sidebar; More on the iPhone): an inbox for app, project and life ideas, the same `nexus-ideas.yaml` as the laptop's Customize › Ideas. Type or dictate one; Claude (Haiku) sorts new ones into topics about 10 minutes after the last (the laptop does it in the background too). **Add an idea to Nexus** for Siri, Shortcuts and the Action button saves one without opening the app; **Open Nexus Ideas** opens it with the keyboard up.
- **Settings › Usage**: the API credits bar adds up what Nexus spent through your key on the laptop, iPad and iPhone (`usage/<device>.json`), and the plan's bars come from the laptop's last reading (`usage/plan.json`).
- **Reminders** (Settings › Reminders), with the laptop's rules: one notice a day for what is due today and tomorrow (key dates a few days early), one shortly before a task planned at a time, and before events that have no alert of their own. They are handed to iOS ahead of time, so they come with the app closed; iOS wakes the app now and then to sync first.
- **A Live Activity on the iPhone and the iPad** (Settings › Lock Screen; the `NexusMobile-Live.ipa` download): one Nexus card on the Lock Screen (and in the iPhone's Dynamic Island). Its big row is a recording (how long, pictures and pins, **Pin**, **Snap**, **Stop**), else the running timer (its task, subject colour, a clock that counts by itself, a bar filling toward the estimate, the tick and **Stop**), else the task up next (a countdown to its time, the tick and **Start**), else "All done for today"; under it the timer while recording, or what comes next (a class from the calendar, a timed task); at its foot how many of today's tasks are done and the time tracked today. Tap it for its task, Recordings or Today. It starts only while Nexus is open on that iPhone or iPad (free signing has no push service), so a timer started on the laptop shows the next time Nexus is opened there; the buttons run in Nexus in the background and update it. "Show my day when nothing runs" (on) keeps it there between timers. The extension (`Widgets/`, target `NexusLiveActivity`) is only in `NexusMobile-Live.ipa` (below), because it needs an App ID of its own; without it Settings has no Lock Screen section.
- **Share into Nexus** from Safari, Photos, Files or Mail, with no share extension (free signing can't carry one): a Shortcut that runs **Save to Nexus** and shows in the Share Sheet (Settings › Share into Nexus walks through making it). A PDF or a photo becomes a board in the notebook Shared (or the one the Shortcut names), a link or words a task in the Inbox; it syncs straight away (NexusCore `Shared`, `Sources/Intents/ShareIntent.swift`).
- **Several windows on the iPad** (side by side or in Stage Manager): **New Window** from the app icon (hold it) or Show All Windows, and **Open in a new window** in a board's menu (hold the board). There is no New window button in the app, so it can't be pressed by accident. Each window has its own task panel, add sheet and Undo.
- **Notebooks**, as on the laptop: `notebooks/<notebook>/<section>/<board>.board` in the data folder, made, renamed and opened from the sidebar. The sidebar shows them as a tree: notebooks, the notebooks inside them (any depth) and their boards, each level with an arrow, in the notebook page's order (OneNote's, from the laptop's import). Tap a board to open it (its row stays selected), so another board is a tap away; the same tree is in the iPhone's Subjects tab. The tree reads only the notebooks it shows, and counts the boards away from the screen, so a folder of thousands of boards (the OneNote import) stays quick.
  - **A board**: PencilKit ink on an endless canvas that grows in every direction, over PDF printouts (pages drawn only near the view, Contents with the PDF's chapters and go to page), images, shapes (rectangle, ellipse, line, arrow) and typed text blocks (markdown, size, colour, background, tick a checkbox with a tap).
  - **Typed blocks use the laptop's rich-text editor** (Settings › Notes › Rich-text editor, on by default): the same ProseMirror editor as the laptop's (`renderer/src/board/richedit.js`), in a web view over the block and in the Writing view, driven by the format bar over the keyboard. Typing `# `, `- `, `[ ] `, `> `, `**bold**`, `==highlight==` formats as on the laptop; chemistry, Review mode, comments, drafts, the Spanish keys and live words from the laptop work as with the earlier editor. Each block is kept as the laptop keeps it: `text/<id>.jsonl` (paragraph ids, who wrote what, the Google Docs link) beside the readable `text/<id>.md` copy, read and written with the laptop's own converters running in JavaScriptCore (NexusCore `RichWords`), so a Google Doc's edits (the hub's bridge writes only the `.jsonl`) reach the iPad and the iPad's reach Google. `tools/richtext/build.mjs` bundles both from the laptop's code: `node build.mjs <path to the nexus repo>` (after `npm ci` there and `npm install --no-save esbuild` here) writes `Packages/NexusCore/Sources/NexusCore/Resources/richcore.js` and `Resources/RichText/rich.html`. Spanish practice opens a block in the earlier editor, which turns the keyboard's checks off.
  - **Tools** (the grid button): the Study tools (Molecules, VSEPR chart, Enthalpies, from the laptop's `.nexus-tools/`) and any .html file in the notebook, used in a panel from the right; put one on the board as a live card (tap it twice to use it again, where you left it) or as a picture. Tools run offline with no access to your files.
  - Fingers pick, move and resize items; hold the Pencil still at the end of a stroke to snap it to a line, circle, ellipse or polygon.
  - Saved in the laptop's board format, byte for byte, 600 ms after an edit: only the tiles that changed are written, and every stroke keeps its id, so a sync merge sees only what changed. Another device's write is read in when nothing here is unsaved.
  - **Search boards**: handwriting read on the iPad with Vision, plus typed text; tap a hit to open the board there. The index lives in the app, never in the data folder.
  - The ink test's own boards were copied once into **Notebooks › Ink test**; the originals stay under Ink test boards.

- **Shortcuts**, editable in **Settings › Shortcuts**, with a **Laptop | iPad** switch: search, collapsible sections, conflicts in red (a board key that takes over an app-wide one is a grey note), Conflicts only, a reset beside each changed key and Reset all.
  - **iPad keys** (with a hardware keyboard; hold ⌘ to see them): ⌘1–6 Today, Planner, Tasks, Notebooks, Inbox, Someday; ⌘, Settings; ⌘N new task; ⌘F search tasks; ⇧⌘N new notebook; ⌘Z undo the last change; on a board ⌘W close, ⌘Z / ⇧⌘Z undo and redo, ⌘= ⌘- ⌘0 zoom, ⌘G PDF contents, P E L pen, eraser, lasso, T text, ↩ edit, ⌫ delete. Tap a key to change it and press the new one, or pick it without a keyboard; a key another shortcut has there offers Swap or Replace.
  - **Gestures** on a board: two-finger tap undo, three-finger tap redo, the Pencil's double-tap and squeeze doing what Settings › Apple Pencil says, each movable to another action or off.
  - Kept in the data folder as `shortcuts/ipad.yaml` (the iPad writes it whole; the laptop may change a `keys:` line). The Laptop side reads `shortcuts/laptop.yaml` and edits just the changed `keys:` line. The format is `docs/data-format.md` "Shortcuts" in the Nexus repo.

## The data folder

The app reads `On My iPad › Nexus › nexus-data` (or a folder picked from Files) in the laptop's format (`docs/data-format.md` in the Nexus repo). Reading never writes. Every task change (waiting on, repeats, tags and the note included) is a line-level edit of one `tasks.yaml` or `inbox.yaml`, checked by parsing the result before it is saved, so comments, order and formatting stay and the laptop sees a one-line change. A workstream's `notes.md` is edited as plain text and saved whole. A file that doesn't parse, a task without an id, and a folder in another format version are read-only. The app looks for changes every few seconds and when it comes back to the front, so sync can write underneath it.

The pure part is the `NexusCore` package (`Packages/NexusCore`): the folder, the YAML edits, the planning model, the board files, shape snapping and capture parsing, with tests on a copy of the laptop's `dev/fixtures/data-vault` and the laptop's own board and shape tests.

## Sync

The iPad keeps its own full copy of the data folder and syncs it with the laptop through the hub on the always-on G14 PC (the protocol is `docs/sync.md` in the Nexus repo). It works offline; while the app is open a change shows on the other device within a few seconds.

To set it up on the iPad:

1. Start the hub on the G14 and sync the laptop with it first (the iPad never fills an empty hub).
2. Install Tailscale on the iPad and sign in with the same account as the G14, or be on the home Wi-Fi.
3. In Nexus, open **Settings › Sync** and fill in the **hub address** (the G14's Tailscale address or name, e.g. `http://100.x.y.z:8743`; without a port, 8743 is used), the **hub token** (the hub prints it on its first start), and **this device's name** (`iPad` unless you have more than one).
4. Turn on **Sync with the hub**. If the iPad's folder is empty, everything is downloaded. If it already has files that differ from the hub's, Nexus asks how to start: **Use the hub's copy** (recommended: the iPad's folder is moved aside to `nexus-data before sync <date>` in On My iPad › Nexus, then everything is downloaded) or **Merge with this iPad's files** (where a file differs, the iPad's version is kept beside it as a conflict copy).

Settings › Sync shows where things stand (Up to date, Syncing, Offline), lists conflict copies (files with `(conflict …)` in their name: open both, keep what you want, delete the copy) and files that are left alone because their name can't be used on Windows. If more than 25 files and more than a quarter of the folder disappear at once, sync stops and asks before deleting them on the hub. A folder without `nexus.yaml` is never synced, so a missing folder can't look like everything was deleted.

The code: `Packages/NexusCore/Sources/NexusCore/Sync*.swift` (the paths, the state, the hub's HTTP protocol and the client's cycle, tested against an in-process hub) and `Sources/Sync/` (running it while the app is open, the token in the Keychain). The sync state lives in Application Support, never in the data folder.

## Building

There is no Mac in the loop: every push builds on a GitHub-hosted Mac (`.github/workflows/build.yml`), running the NexusCore tests (when the push changed `Packages/`) and then building the app, in one job. The project file is generated from `project.yml` with XcodeGen, and the build is unsigned. `main` publishes **Releases › latest › NexusMobile.ipa**; any other branch publishes **Releases › preview-<branch>**.

**Mac jobs are scarce.** The account may run only 5 GitHub-hosted Mac jobs at once, shared by every branch, so most of a build's time is spent queued, not building (Nov 2026: 6 minutes of work, 30 to 70 minutes of waiting when several branches push). When you write or change a workflow:

- One Mac job per check. Build for testing once, then run the iPad and the iPhone simulators one after the other in the same job (as `ui-check.yml` does), never as a matrix or two jobs.
- Give a push trigger a `paths:` filter, so a check runs only when its screens or tests change.
- `ui-check.yml` runs every UI test (iPad, then every `…PhoneTests` class on the iPhone) on every push that changes the app or the tests, so a new feature's tests need no workflow of their own. A check written for one feature (like the old `claude-check.yml`) folds into `ui-check.yml` or is deleted before the branch merges, so main doesn't carry it.
- Push in batches rather than commit by commit: each push to a branch cancels that branch's older runs, but they've often already held a Mac.
- Don't add `pull_request` triggers: the builds run on pushes, and the repo is public.

Every CI or build failure on the iPad or iPhone gets its cause and fix written in [docs/ci-lessons.md](docs/ci-lessons.md); read it before pushing.

## Installing

Install `NexusMobile.ipa` on the iPad with [Sideloadly](https://sideloadly.io) on Windows, signed with a free Apple ID (a spare one is best). Free signing lasts 7 days; Sideloadly renews it automatically when the iPad is awake on the same Wi-Fi as the PC running it. Installing a new build over the old one keeps the app's files.

Each build has two downloads: **`NexusMobile-Live.ipa`**, the app with the Live Activity extension, for the iPhone and the iPad (`PlugIns/NexusLiveActivity.appex`, bundle `app.nexus.mobile.live`). Under free signing the extension needs a second App ID (a free Apple ID holds 10, each for a week) and likely takes a second of the device's 3 sideloaded-app slots; Sideloadly registers and renews it with the app. Re-signing with an existing profile (zsign) can't carry it: the extension needs its own profile, so install that one through Sideloadly. **`NexusMobile.ipa`** is the same app without it (no app extension, one App ID): the one the G14 hub fetches, and the one to use when only a zsign re-sign is possible.
